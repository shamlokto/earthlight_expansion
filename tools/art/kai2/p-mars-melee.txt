16-bit Super Famicom era pixel art sprite sheet of close-combat moves for the exact same mecha as in the reference sprite sheet (the "Mars Kai": white armour with magenta-pink stripes and gold trim, cyan visor, a long beam rifle with a glowing cyan beam bayonet under the barrel), drawn at the same size and in the same style. A 3x3 grid of nine separate sprites, every mecha in strict side view FACING LEFT, generous empty space between them, nothing overlapping:
row 1: (1) wind-up: rifle drawn far back at the hip, bayonet glowing, body coiled to lunge; (2) bayonet lunge: lunging far forward to the left, the rifle thrust straight out, the cyan bayonet stabbing forward; (3) pulling back: rifle drawn back and up, body turning for a second strike.
row 2: (4) rising slash: the rifle swung upward in a diagonal arc, bayonet sweeping up in front; (5) recoiling defensively after a clash, leaning back, rifle held up crosswise to block; (6) hovering forward on thrusters, rifle held angled forward with the bayonet ready.
row 3: (7) a cyan crescent-shaped energy slash arc effect alone, no mecha, glowing cyan with a white-hot inner edge, the curve opening to the right; (8) a cyan starburst impact spark effect alone, no mecha; (9) point-blank shot: rifle jammed forward to the left with a huge bright cyan-white muzzle blast.
The bayonet is always a glowing cyan energy blade with a white-hot core. Clean dark outline, cel shading with 4 tones per colour, crisp limited palette. Plain flat solid muted lavender-blue background, no text, no labels, no numbers, no grid lines, no border, no shadows.
